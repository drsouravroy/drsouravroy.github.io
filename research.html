Research Focus

Understanding protein structure, dynamics, and host-pathogen mechanisms to guide therapeutic discovery.

A big-picture scientific program linking mechanism, computation, and translation

The research program integrates computational modeling, structural analysis, and biologically grounded hypothesis generation to understand disease-relevant proteins and support therapeutic discovery.

Research Philosophy

Drug discovery works best when molecular mechanism and biological context stay connected

Modern therapeutic discovery depends on more than identifying a target. It requires a mechanistic view of how proteins fold, move, bind, and respond to their biological environment. Computational biophysics and structural biology provide a framework for understanding those processes with precision.

In this research program, molecular mechanism, host-pathogen biology, and computational design are treated as part of the same scientific workflow. The goal is to move from biological question to structural interpretation, from structural interpretation to design hypothesis, and from design hypothesis to experimentally meaningful therapeutic insight.

Molecular detail matters most when it improves biological explanation and sharpens the next therapeutic question.

Major Research Areas

Six interconnected research directions

A. Computational Biophysics and Protein Dynamics

Research in this area examines protein folding, conformational transitions, energy landscapes, and structure-function relationships using computational analysis and molecular interpretation.

B. Lyme Disease Biology and Host-Pathogen Interaction

Work on Borrelia burgdorferi proteins addresses virulence-linked functions, host interaction, complement evasion, and the molecular strategies that support pathogen persistence.

C. Structural Biology and Drug Discovery

Structural analysis informs target understanding, binding-site interpretation, inhibitor design, and therapeutic hypothesis generation across disease-relevant systems.

D. Complement Therapeutics

Complement pathway regulation, protease inhibition, immune evasion, and the therapeutic implications of complement biology form an important translational research theme.

E. AI-Powered Peptide and Small-Molecule Discovery

Computational and AI-guided models are used to support target-specific peptide discovery and small-molecule design with mechanistic interpretability in view.

F. Protein-Protein Interactions in Bacterial Systems

Protein interaction studies in non-cholera Vibrio and related systems provide insight into functional networks, bacterial adaptation, and biologically relevant interfaces.

Future Directions

Where the research program can grow

Future work will continue to integrate computational and experimental biology more closely, especially in areas where molecular mechanism can sharpen therapeutic design decisions. This includes AI-assisted drug discovery, improved structure-function mapping, and better mechanistic models for infectious disease proteins.

A consistent emphasis will remain on therapeutically relevant host-pathogen systems, complement biology, and target-focused discovery strategies that stay grounded in structural and biophysical interpretation.

Forward-Looking Themes

  • Integrative computational-experimental biology
  • AI-assisted drug discovery
  • Structure-function mapping
  • Therapeutic targeting of host-pathogen systems
  • Mechanistic models for infectious disease proteins

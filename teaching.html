Teaching & Mentoring

Interdisciplinary scientific training across molecules, mechanisms, and computation

Academic mentoring at the interface of biophysics, structural biology, and discovery science

Teaching and mentoring interests emphasize rigorous thinking, computational fluency, biological mechanism, and the ability to connect quantitative analysis with meaningful research questions.

Teaching Philosophy

Training should make students stronger at asking and framing scientific questions

Dr. Roy’s teaching philosophy is grounded in interdisciplinary training. Students should be able to move between molecular biophysics, structural interpretation, computational reasoning, and literature-driven biological hypothesis building.

The goal is not only technical competence, but also scientific judgment: understanding when a model is useful, how structural data should be interpreted, and how computational observations can support better biological questions.

Training Priorities

  • Mechanistic thinking
  • Reproducible computational practice
  • Clear scientific communication
  • Cross-disciplinary fluency
